# VBC00001 — Syntax error

**Severity:** Error &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Family:** `VBC` (parser / concrete syntax tree) &nbsp;·&nbsp;
**Source metadata:** [`VBSyntaxErrorInfo`](https://rubberduck-vba.github.io/RDCore.SDK/api/RDCore.SDK.Model.Errors.VBSyntaxErrorInfo.html)

A token was reached that the RD-VBA grammar cannot place in the current context. This is the parser's
**general fallback**: when the input cannot be parsed and no more specific code applies, it reports
`VBC00001`. Recurring shapes of syntax error are promoted from `VBC00001` to their own code in the
`VBC00042`–`VBC00999` range.

The diagnostic's verbose detail says what MS-VBA says when it compiles the module, and its range is
where MS-VBA puts the caret. In the body of a procedure, that is `Syntax error`, over the whole line:
from its start (its indentation, label or line number) to its last character, its comment and the
lines it continues on included. In a procedure's header, or in a declaration of the module, it is
`Expected: …`, at the token where what MS-VBA expected is missing, or right where the line ends.

## How it is raised

|Situation|What the verbose detail says|Resolution|
|---|---|---|
|A statement in a procedure's body cannot be read, e.g. a misplaced `Then`, a doubled operator, an unbalanced bracket, an operator with no right operand, a declaration inside a procedure body, or an expression used as a statement.|`Syntax error`, over the whole line|Put the tokens in a legal order, or move or remove the construct.|
|A `ByVal` before an argument of a `RaiseEvent` (**MS-VBAL §5.4.2.20** gives the statement no `ByVal`, and **§5.6.13.1** allows one only in the invocation of an external procedure; for any other call, see [VBC09327](vbc09327.md)).|that a `RaiseEvent` argument cannot be written with `ByVal`, at the argument|Remove the `ByVal`.|
|A procedure's header or a declaration of the module is missing a part, e.g. `Sub ()`, `Sub S(x As)`, `Sub S(x`, `Property X()`, `Private Type`, `Declare PtrSafe Sub Foo`, `Option Compare`, `Private x As`.|MS-VBA's message: `Expected: identifier`, `Expected: type name`, `Expected: New or type name`, `Expected: list separator or )`, `Expected: expression`, `Expected: end of statement`, `Expected: Get or Let or Set`, `Expected: Lib`, or `Expected: Text or Binary`, at the token MS-VBA puts the caret on. Where the grammar does not tell what was expected, `Syntax error`, at the token.|Complete the declaration.|
|A conditional-compilation directive line that is not well formed, e.g. `#If VBA7` with no `Then`, `#End If junk`, `#Iff X Then`, `#Const X As Long = 1`. The line is no directive, and opens, continues or ends no block ([**RD-VBAL §2.6.1**](../specs/rd-vbal.2.6.1.syntax-errors.md#directives-that-are-wrong)).|MS-VBA's message: `Expected: Then`, `Expected: expression`, `Expected: end of statement`, `Expected: =`, `Expected: identifier`, or `Expected: If or Else or ElseIf or End or EndIf or Const`, at the token MS-VBA puts the caret on|Complete or correct the directive; write a comment after it with `'`.|
|A stray or unterminated token, e.g. an unclosed string, an unexpected line continuation, a character outside any token.|in a procedure's body, `Syntax error`, over the whole line; outside it, what MS-VBA expected there, at the token|Remove or complete the token.|
|The module is nested more deeply than the parser can read at all: its rules nest more than 32,000 deep, a limit of RD-VBA's parse and not of the language ([**RD-VBAL §2.6.1**](../specs/rd-vbal.2.6.1.syntax-errors.md#fallback-and-dedicated-codes)). That is an expression or a block nested in thousands of others, such as 32,000 parentheses or 5,300 nested calls, past the 2,500 levels of [VBC00060](vbc00060.md). The parse stops, and nothing of the module is analyzed; a syntax error found before it stopped is reported with it.|`This module is nested too deeply to be read`, at the start of the module|Split the deepest part into smaller ones, for example by writing parts of the expression to variables.|
|A `Tab` or `Spc` clause in a list to print, right before `=` or `&`, e.g. `Debug.Print Tab(2) = 5`, `Debug.Print Spc(2) & "x"`. The clause moves the print position, and is no value to compare or join. MS-VBA refuses these too. A `+` or a `-` after a space is no error: it begins the next item. So `Debug.Print Tab(2) + 1` prints 1 after the clause, and the VBE writes it `Tab(2); 1`.|`Syntax error`, over the whole line|To print a value after the clause, write a `;` between them: `Debug.Print Spc(2); "x"`.|
|`Debug` is used other than as `Debug.Print` or `Debug.Assert`, e.g. `Set Debug = Nothing`, `x = Debug`, `With Debug`, `Call Debug.Print("x")`. `Debug` belongs to the host, and is only ever written as those two statements.|`Debug`|Write `Debug.Print …` or `Debug.Assert …` as a statement of its own.|
|A parameter is named with a reserved word, e.g. `Sub S(ByVal Mod As Long)`, `Sub S(ByVal Scale As Long)`. A reserved word (**MS-VBAL §3.3.5.2**) cannot name a parameter (**MS-VBAL §5.3.1.5**), and MS-VBA refuses these too. The rest of the procedure is still analyzed.|`Expected: identifier. 'Mod' is a reserved word and cannot name a parameter.`, at the name|Rename the parameter.|

## Notes

The RD-VBA grammar is permissive at the token level by design, and defers meaning to the semantic
layers. A `VBC00001` is therefore always a *structural* problem: the text could not be assembled into a
syntax tree at all.

Conditions that need symbol information (an undefined name, a duplicate declaration) are **not** syntax
errors. They are reported later, as
[semantic compilation errors](../specs/rd-vbal.2.6.2.semantic-compilation-errors.md).
