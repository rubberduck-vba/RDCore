# RDC11018 — Integer arithmetic of Abs, Int or Fix that can overflow

**Severity:** Warning &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Family:** `RDC` (Rubberduck Core analyzers) &nbsp;·&nbsp;
**Raised by:** [`ArgumentTypedArithmeticAnalyzer`](../api/RDCore.Diagnostics.Analyzers.ArgumentTypedArithmeticAnalyzer.html)

An addition, a subtraction or a multiplication of whole numbers uses `Abs`, `Int` or `Fix` of a number, and can overflow where the language specification
says it would not.

MS-VBAL declares `Abs`, `Int` and `Fix` `As Variant`. MS-VBA gives the call the type of the number it is given instead: `Fix(n)` of an `Integer` is an
`Integer`. So `Fix(n) * 7000`, with `n` = 5, multiplies two `Integer` values, and the result does not fit in one: run-time error 6 (Overflow). Had the call been a
`Variant`, as MS-VBAL declares it, the product would have widened to a `Long` and been 35000 (recorded in Excel, as vb6.exe does).

RD-VBA does as MS-VBA does, and warns where the difference can show.

## How it is raised

|Situation|Example|Resolution|
|---|---|---|
|`+`, `-` or `*` of `Byte`, `Integer` or `Long` values, one of which is `Abs`, `Int` or `Fix` of a variable of one of those types.|`Dim n As Integer` … `x = Fix(n) * 7000`|Convert to a larger type before the operation: `CLng(Fix(n)) * 7000`, or declare `n` as a `Long`.|

## Notes

A call given a `Variant`, a `String`, a `Boolean` or a `Date` is a `Variant`, as MS-VBAL declares it, and is not reported. Neither is an operation of `Single`,
`Double` or `Currency` values, which overflows as a `Variant` operation would.

`Sgn` returns an `Integer` and `Asc` an `Integer`, as MS-VBAL declares them: an `Integer` operation of either is what the specification says, and is not
reported.
